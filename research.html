Research

Research

My papers span housing and land markets, household choices, labor, and the environment. I list working papers first, followed by published articles. Please email me if you would like the latest version of a paper that is not publicly posted.

Working papers

Titles reflect the latest manuscripts provided in October–November 2026; journal statuses are as reported in the October 2026 CV.

01

Migration Restrictions and the Migrant-Native Wage Gap: The Roles of Wage Setting and Sorting

Naijia Guo, Li Zhang, Ben Zou, and Rongjie Zhang

Revise & resubmit, American Economic Review

Studies how migration restrictions contribute to the migrant–native wage gap through firms’ wage-setting decisions and workers’ sorting across employers.

Working paper ↗
02

When Protection Hurts: Labor Market Consequences of Mandatory Heat Allowances in China

Sumit Agarwal, Kam C. Chan, Nianhang Xu, Ruoming Yang, and Rongjie Zhang

Revise & resubmit, Journal of Environmental Economics and Management

Using matched employer–employee records, we find that a mandatory heat allowance was partly offset by lower wages and greater dismissal risk, particularly at private firms.

03

Sex Ratios and Competitive Human Capital Investment: Evidence from Housing Prices and Educational Expenditures

Shang-Jin Wei, Jing Wu, and Rongjie Zhang

Working paper

In cities with more male-skewed marriage markets, families pay higher premiums for access to elite primary schools and spend more on children’s education, especially for sons.

04

Intra-household Bargaining and Fertility Decision-making: Evidence from Homeownership Structure and Housing Price Shocks in China

Boyuan Huang, Jing Wu, and Rongjie Zhang

Working paper

Housing-price shocks shift the wealth gap between spouses; a larger gap in the husband’s favor is associated with higher birth probabilities, particularly among couples with daughters but no sons.

05

Elite Platforms or Big Fish? Sorting, Human Capital Accumulation, and Returns to Top University Admission

Rongjie Zhang and Wenlan Qian

Working paper

Linking university admission cutoffs to long-run earnings records, we trace a large entry-wage premium from elite enrollment and distinguish early employer sorting from later within-firm advancement.

06

Defending the Coasts: Seawalls, Typhoons, and Adapting to Sea-Level Rise

Yichun Fan, Yuqi Song, and Rongjie Zhang

Working paper

Studies how seawall protection is valued in coastal housing and land markets, and how that value relates to exposure to typhoons and rising seas.

More valuable parental housing is associated with greater emotional support from adult children in multi-child households, consistent with an informal bequest-based eldercare arrangement.
